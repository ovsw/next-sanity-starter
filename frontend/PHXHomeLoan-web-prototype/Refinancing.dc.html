THE BASICS

What does home loan refinancing mean?

When you refinance your home loan, you essentially replace your current mortgage with a new one. The new lender pays off the former and offers you a new principal and refinance rate — an excellent way to get a personalized home loan that is more affordable.

People refinance for several reasons: lowering monthly payments, shortening the loan term, opting for more favorable terms and conditions, or taking advantage of the equity gained on the property.

THE PROCESS — 5 STEPS

How does refinancing a home work?

Like buying a new home, refinancing requires you to apply, get an appraisal of the house, and then close your loan. Technical elements like underwriting and locking in an interest rate are handled by your loan specialist. Typically it takes 30–45 days to complete — confirm the timeline with your lender.

  1. 1

    Submit an application

    You can stay with your current lender or shop around

    The process starts with applying with your lender — who could be your initial lender or a different one. You are at liberty to shop around for a better refinance option, and whoever your home loan lender is will pay off your current loan.

    The lender will ask for the same information you supplied when you bought the home: your income, assets, debts (auto loan, credit card, student loans, etc.), recent bank statements, and credit score. If you are married, your lender may request your spouse's documents, and you might need recent tax returns ready if asked for additional documentation.

  2. 2

    Underwriting

    Your lender verifies everything you submitted

    Underwriting begins soon after you submit your application. At this stage, your lender reviews the information and documents you supplied to make sure everything checks out, including an appraisal to determine the value of the house.

    If you are refinancing to lower your monthly payment, your home's value could afford you enough equity to get rid of private mortgage insurance — or make you eligible for a different kind of home loan with other long-term financial benefits.

  3. 3

    Home appraisal

    An estimate of your home's current value

    Your lender will order a house appraisal so they can approve you for a refinance loan. The appraiser visits your property, and afterward you'll receive an estimate of your home's value. To ensure it reflects the actual value, tidy up the property, fix any pending upgrades, and list out any upgrades you've implemented since you bought the home.

    The appraisal helps determine what loan options are available to you — whether you'll get the loan amount you want, pay the difference out of pocket if your home's value is less than the loan amount, or reduce how much you're requesting.

  4. 4

    Locking in your interest rate

    Rate locks typically last 15–60 days

    Once approved, your lender may give you the option to lock your interest rate in case of fluctuations before the loan closes. Rate locks typically last 15–60 days. Alternatively, you can float your rate — you might get a lower one, but there's a chance you set yourself up for a higher rate instead.

  5. 5

    Closing the new loan

    Usually faster than closing a home purchase

    After underwriting, you can close your loan. The Closing Disclosure, a document your lender provides, shows you all the numbers before you sign. You may have to pay closing costs unless your lender bundles them into your new mortgage loan.

    After closing, you have a grace period of 3 days to cancel if something goes wrong and you do not wish to continue.

FOUR GOOD REASONS

Why refinancing your home loan is a good option

01

Change your loan term or reduce the monthly payment

If you have a 30-year loan and wish to pay it off faster, refinance to a shorter term like 15 years — this increases your monthly payment but reduces the overall interest you pay. You can also lengthen your loan term to lower your monthly payment.

02

Take advantage of home equity

With a cash-out refinance, you can borrow more than you owe on your home and keep the difference. If upgrades increased your home's value, you can use that equity toward home repairs, debt consolidation, and other expenses.

03

Lower your interest rate

Interest rates are constantly changing, so it's wise to refinance to a cheaper rate and save significant money in the long run. Even a slight percentage change, with the help of an experienced lender, will significantly lower your total interest over the loan's life.

04

Change your loan type

If you started with an adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) to save on interest, you can refinance to a fixed-rate mortgage when rates are low. You can also refinance an FHA loan to escape mortgage insurance premiums by switching to a different loan type.

OTHER REFINANCE STRUCTURES
TAP YOUR EQUITY

Cash-Out Refinance

If you have equity in your home, you can refinance for an amount higher than your current mortgage and take the difference in cash — for home improvement, debt consolidation, and other expenses.

MINIMAL UPFRONT FEES

Low Upfront Cost Refinance

Refinance without paying as much out of pocket at closing, by accepting a slightly higher interest rate or by folding certain costs into the new loan, which can still make sense if the new terms are better than your current mortgage.
