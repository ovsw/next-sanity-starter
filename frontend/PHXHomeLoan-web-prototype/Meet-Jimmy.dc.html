THE EARLY YEARS

The son of a salesman, forged as a Marine

Jimmy grew up in the San Francisco Bay area — his dad a produce salesman, his mom a waitress. His parents divorced when he was five, and he spent his childhood bouncing back and forth between two homes. Nothing, by his own telling, that marked him as special.

But one conviction took hold early and never left: how you make people feel matters more than how much you know. At 18, he acted on it — enlisting in the Marine Corps infantry, deploying to Iraq in support of Operation Iraqi Freedom, and traveling the world along the way.

“Deep down, I always believed that how you made people feel matters more than how much you know.”

A NEW MISSION

A girl from Arizona, and a new way to serve

One night in Hawaii — right after losing his grandmother — Jimmy met a woman named Sylvia who was there on vacation. She had lost her mother shortly before. Neither of them was supposed to be on that island. The next morning he called his sister and told her, “I just met my future wife.” She asked how he could know. He said, “I just know.”

He was right. Jimmy finished his service, packed his bags for Arizona, and married her — twenty years and two children later, they're still going. That same season, a conversation with his sister-in-law pointed him toward a new way to keep serving: a career in mortgages.

“Once a Marine, always a Marine!”
Jimmy's favorite phrase — and the standard he brings to every family he serves
DISCIPLINE, APPLIED

Lessons from the Corps, applied to lending

There was one small problem when his sister-in-law suggested mortgages: no degree, no finance background, no connections. But Marines adapt, improvise, and overcome. Jimmy walked door-to-door down Camelback Road — mortgage company to mortgage company — until one of them finally said, “We'll give you a shot, Marine.”

For years, while everyone around him made six figures, Jimmy barely made minimum wage. He'll be the first to say he was never the smartest guy in the room — but he could teach. So he founded a real estate school and started a YouTube channel, and the discipline he learned in the Corps has since carried into an award-winning lending career.
